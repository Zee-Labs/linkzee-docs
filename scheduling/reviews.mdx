Social proof sells. After each appointment, Linkzee asks your client for a
quick review — your **average rating with stars** appears where new visitors
decide, and the **Reviews** tab turns that feedback into conversations.

<Frame caption="Your average rating shows right where visitors pick a service.">
  <img src="/images/scheduling/booking-wizard-services-en.webp" alt="Linkzee screenshot" />
</Frame>

## How reviews are collected

About **2 hours after an appointment ends**, the client gets a review request —
by **WhatsApp** (when your WhatsApp channel is active) or by **email**. The
link opens a simple page: **1 to 5 stars** and an optional comment. No account,
no friction.

<Note>
  Only people who **actually booked** can review — each review link belongs to
  one appointment, and each appointment can be reviewed **once**. No fake
  reviews, no duplicates.
</Note>

## The Reviews tab

Open **Scheduling → Reviews**. The top of the page is your reputation at a
glance: **average rating**, how many reviews you have, and the breakdown from
5 to 1 star.

Next to it sits the number that matters most: how many reviews **need
attention**.

### Reviews that need attention

Any review of **3 stars or less** that you haven't followed up on yet lands in
this queue. It is not there to be hidden — it is there to be **answered**.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the attention filter">
    Click the **Attention** chip (or the counter at the top) to see only the
    reviews still waiting for you.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Talk to the client">
    Use the **WhatsApp** or **email** button on the review. The WhatsApp
    message comes pre-written for the situation — you can edit it before
    sending.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Mark as handled">
    Once you've spoken to the person, click the check icon. The review leaves
    the queue and gets a **Handled** badge.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  A client who complains and gets a call back often comes back. The queue
  exists so no unhappy review goes unanswered — and so you can see, in one
  place, what keeps going wrong.
</Tip>

### Finding a specific review

Filter by **rating**, **service**, **professional**, or search by the client's
name. The chips above the list switch between **All**, **Attention**,
**Featured** and **Hidden**. Long lists load in pages — click **Load more** at
the bottom.

Filtering by professional answers a question that is hard to see otherwise:
whether one person on your team is pulling the average up or down.

### What each review shows

| Item | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Stars and comment | Exactly what the client wrote — never editable |
| Client's name | The name used in the booking |
| Contact | WhatsApp and email buttons to talk to that client |
| Service | Which service was reviewed |
| Professional | Who took the appointment, when you have a team |
| Date | When the review came in |

## Featuring and hiding

Two actions control what the public sees:

- **Feature** (star icon) — the review goes to the front of the
  [Reviews block](/your-page/reviews-block) on your page.
- **Hide** (eye icon) — the review stops counting toward your public average
  and never appears on your page. It stays visible to you, and you can show it
  again at any time.

<Note>
  Reviews can be hidden, but never **edited or deleted** — that is what keeps
  your rating trustworthy for visitors. Hiding also does not remove a review
  from the attention queue: the conversation with that client still matters.
</Note>

## Where your rating shows

- On your **booking page**, as the average and count (for example ⭐ 4.9 · 23),
  right where visitors pick a service.
- On your **Linkzee page**, if you add the
  [Reviews block](/your-page/reviews-block) — with the reviews themselves, not
  just the number.
