A professional can take bookings in more than one business on Linkzee: their own
calendar and a team they work with, for example. **Linked calendars** prevent
someone from booking, on one business's page, a time the professional already has
in the other.

- A booking in **any** of the places shows as busy in the others, right away.
- Each business only sees **"Busy elsewhere"** — never the client's name, the
  service, the price, nor the other business's name.
- Only the professional sees, on their own calendar, where the busy time comes
  from ("Busy · Other business name").

## How to link

A link needs **two yeses**: the business's and the professional's. Either side can
start.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="From the business">
    <Steps>
      <Step title="Open the Team">
        Go to **Scheduling → Team** and open the staff member's card. The **Linked
        calendar** row appears once they accepted the [team invite](/scheduling/team).
      </Step>
      <Step title="Ask to link">
        Tap **Link**. The professional gets a push, an e-mail and a card on their
        **Today** tab.
      </Step>
      <Step title="They accept">
        When accepting, they say which calendar is theirs ("I attend this
        calendar"). The row turns **Linked** by itself.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="From the professional">
    <Steps>
      <Step title="Open your calendars">
        Go to **Settings → Integrations & privacy → Linked calendars**. The section
        lists every place where you take bookings on Linkzee.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Say which calendar is yours">
        On your own business, turn on **I attend this calendar**. If the business
        has a team, pick **which staff member you are**.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Ask the teams to link">
        On each team, tap **Link**. The owner gets the request on the team card
        and on their Today tab.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

<Note>
  Before accepting, the professional sees how many times already overlap in the
  next 30 days. **Nothing is cancelled**: overlapping appointments are marked with a
  triangle on both calendars, to be rescheduled calmly.
</Note>

## What crosses between calendars

| On one calendar | Shows on the other as |
| --- | --- |
| A booking (pending or confirmed), from any channel | **Busy**, with the service's buffer. Visitors simply don't see the time. |
| A cancelled or rescheduled booking | The time frees up (or moves) right away. |
| A block (meeting, lunch, day off) | **Nothing.** A block means "I'm not selling my time here now" — often because the person is at the other place. |
| Business hours, holidays, Google Calendar | Nothing. Each business decides when it sells; only time already sold crosses. |

## In the day-to-day calendar

- **Timeline**: time busy on another calendar shows with a dotted texture (blocks
  are stripes). Tapping it shows a notice instead of opening a booking.
- **Team mode**: busy time from another calendar shows when you filter by a staff
  member.
- **Conflicts**: an appointment overlapping the other calendar gets a triangle and
  a line in its details. Nothing is cancelled for you.
- **Booking outside hours** respects the other calendar; **recording a past
  appointment** does not.

## Unlinking

Either side can unlink, with a confirmation. The other side is notified.
**No appointment is cancelled**: those already booked keep blocking the
professional's calendar until they happen.

The link also ends by itself when the professional's team access is removed,
when the staff member is removed, or when the business changes its scheduling
mode. An unanswered request expires in **30 days**; a declined request can only be
repeated after **30 days**.

## Privacy

A link is an exchange between two businesses authorised by the professional.
Each side consents once, to the text it saw, and can step back at any time. All
that crosses is "busy from 10:00 to 11:00": the other business learns that you
are busy at those times, with no names, services or prices. People inside a team
may notice a colleague had activity elsewhere on a day, never what.
